Apartment studio

Your floor plan, as a website

Drop the floor plan your architect gave you. You get its plan page with every room's size, a 3D you can walk through at true size, and the sun crossing your rooms, ready to put online.

Your drawing stays on your computer. Everything happens in this page, with the same tools that built the sample: the drawing is read and the site is built by your own browser, and nothing is uploaded. The first time, the page loads its Python tools (about 35 MB, and 9 MB more to open a DWG or 17 MB to read a PDF); your browser keeps them for next time.
1

Your drawing

The DWG straight from AutoCAD; a DXF from Revit, ArchiCAD or most other CAD programs (Save As or Export, then DXF); or a PDF printed from CAD, with the walls filled in. A scan or a photo of a plan can't be read.

2

Where it is

For the sun. Find the latitude and longitude by right-clicking the building on a map. Leave them empty and the plan and 3D are still built, with the sun of the sample's site.

The top of the drawing faces a compass bearing: 0 north, 90 east, 180 south, 270 west. Look at the building on a map and see which way the side at the top of the drawing looks. The clock is your time zone in hours from UTC; left empty, it is worked out from the longitude.

3

Read it, and check what was found

    On the check page below, the red dashed outlines are the drawing's own walls: they should sit on the black. Then look at every door, window and room name. Under the plan, click a door to change which way it opens, and give the real window sills and heights and the ceiling: the drawing can't say them.

    Take it with you
    
      
    
      
    The zip is the whole website: upload the folder to any web host, or to GitHub Pages. The corrections made on the check page are in both. To change anything else (a wall, a room's name) and keep the site up to date, use the template with Claude Code: put apartment.json in your copy and ask it to "build my apartment".
    
    
    
  
    

  
    Apartment studio: your drawing in, a site out. The drawing tools run in your browser through Pyodide; three.js draws the 3D. DWG files are opened by LibreDWG, free software under the GNU GPL v3 (source); PDFs by PyMuPDF, under the GNU AGPL v3, as Pyodide packages it.
